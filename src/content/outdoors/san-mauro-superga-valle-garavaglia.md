Twelve and a half kilometres and 619 metres of climbing on foot, from San Mauro up through the woods to Superga and on over the hills to the Valle Garavaglia, in May.

The way leaves the square by the old brick tower and climbs at once into the wood, on lanes between green banks and then on steep paths and earth steps, with the leaves just out and the city glimpsed through the trees. Along the long brick wall below the basilica the view opens up: the whole city and the river below, and the Alps still white with snow beyond the plain.

From Superga the way goes east along the hills on narrow paths through the woods, down gullies and past old walls, a small castellated house behind a wall and a fallen trunk carved with letters. In the last part, down towards the Valle Garavaglia, water was running down the paths between the bushes.
