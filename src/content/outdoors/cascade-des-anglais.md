Three kilometres and 101 metres of climbing on foot, from the Col de Vizzavona down to the Cascade des Anglais and back, in the middle of August.

The way runs through the forest of Vizzavona, on paths among the roots of tall beeches, and comes out on open slopes of scree and pale rock below the peaks. The Agnone tumbles down the valley over slabs of granite in a chain of small falls, with clear green and turquoise pools between the boulders, under the pines.

The falls are named after the English. Walking sites agree that the name goes back to the nineteenth century, when British visitors, drawn by the cool climate of Vizzavona, came here to bathe in the cold water of the Agnone.
