Thirteen kilometres and 156 metres of climbing on foot, from Cala Domingos along the east coast of Mallorca to Cala Magraner and back, on a grey and cloudy day in the middle of August.

The way leaves the resort on a promenade above the sea and soon turns into a path along the cliffs, with wooden rails and rock steps above the first coves. Past the last houses the coast goes wild: a flat plateau of pitted limestone and dry scrub, cut every so often by long narrow inlets with turquoise water and pebble beaches at their heads.

Between Cala Domingos and Cala Magraner there are several of these coves. A German hiking guide lists Cala Bota, Cala Virgili and Cala Pilota along the way, and Beach Atlas describes Cala Magraner, Cala Pilota and Cala Virgili as three narrow bays opening off the same stretch of coast. None of them can be reached by car, only on foot, so they stay much wilder than the beaches of the resorts, and a single sailboat lay at anchor in one of them.

On the way back rain clouds gathered over the scrubland and the track came down to the sandy beach of Cala Domingos, where a stream runs out across the sand.
