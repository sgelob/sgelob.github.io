Fifty-two kilometres and 1,688 metres of climbing on the cross-country bike, east over the hills into the Monferrato as far as Pino d’Asti and Albugnano, and back by another way.

Fog lay over the hill, the last of the snow still in lumps along the verge, and it stayed with me past Pavarolo and Marentino, where the fields beside the road were white with frost.

Below Marentino the route follows the bank of the lake of Arignano, the largest stretch of water on the Turin hill. Count Paolo Remigio Costa della Trinità had it dammed at the end of the 1830s to water the fields and drive two mills, and it gave ice for the summer too. Drained in 1980, it was filled again in 2007, and its reeds now draw birdwatchers.

Past Arignano and Moriondo, narrow lanes climb through cuttings and between bare vineyards to Pino d’Asti, a village of some two hundred people under its castle, and on to Albugnano, the highest point of the ride, where the fog hid everything beyond the square.

The way back is where the dirt began: gravel between the vines below Vezzolano, a grassy track still white with frost, then the sunken lanes round Moncucco, cut deep into the soft hillside and turned to mud, with branches down across the way. After Sciolze the last climb went over the hill by Bardassano, on a lane lined with acanthus past an old church.
