Eleven kilometres and barely 117 metres of climbing on foot, round the lakes of Plitvice, the oldest national park in Croatia, on a grey and misty summer day.

The sixteen lakes step down one into the next in a wooded valley, held back by barriers of travertine that the water itself builds up as it flows, so that every lake spills into the one below over curtains of falling water. Their colour shifts from turquoise to emerald with the light and the depth.

We walked the wooden boardwalks laid just above the water, along the shores and across the falls, under limestone cliffs with caves in them and moss hanging from the rock. The electric boat crosses Kozjak, the largest of the lakes, between low cloud and wooded hills, and the paths beyond it run through the woods past one wall of waterfalls after another.

The park was founded in 1949 and has been a UNESCO World Heritage Site since 1979.
