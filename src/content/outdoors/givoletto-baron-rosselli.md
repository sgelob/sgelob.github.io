Thirteen kilometres and 925 metres of climbing on foot, from Givoletto up the ridge to Monte Rosselli and round over Monte Baron, on the first mountains above the plain north-west of Turin.

It was still late winter on the slopes: long sweeps of pale dry grass, bare oaks and birches, a few dark pines, and higher up patches of ice in the grass and frost on the needles. The old path climbs out of the village along the slope, with the valley below, and on the hillside opposite a row of half-moon scars stood out in the dry grass.

Monte Rosselli, about 1,000 metres, has a broad bare top that walkers on Gulliver say looks out over Turin; from the ridge the plain lay in the haze on one side, and on the other the dark ridges of the Lanzo valleys rose under a grey sky to the snowy peaks beyond. Monte Baron, about 820 metres, has a small hut with benches on its summit among the trees.

The way down drops over the pale slopes dotted with pines and back to Givoletto.
