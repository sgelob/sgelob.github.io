Fourteen kilometres and 1,114 metres of climbing on foot, from Andalo to the top of the Paganella and back, in August.

The way climbs through the spruce forest and then on gravel tracks through the dwarf pines, and the higher it goes the more the Brenta Dolomites fill the view across the valley: a long line of jagged pale peaks above the green meadows. The summit, at 2,125 metres, is a grassy dome crowned with antennas.

From up there the view goes all round, according to the local tourist board: the Brenta to the west, the valley of the Adige deep below to the east, and on clear days Lake Garda to the south. Since 2009 the Dolomites, the Brenta among them, have been a UNESCO World Heritage Site.
