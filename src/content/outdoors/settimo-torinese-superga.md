Twenty-one kilometres and 493 metres of climbing on foot, from Settimo across the plain to San Mauro and up to the basilica of Superga, and back, at the beginning of April.

The first part is flat: gravel lanes across the meadows with the hill of Superga rising ahead under the clouds, a tractor at work in a field, and the river crossed at San Mauro. From Sant’Anna the way climbs through light spring woods, the trees just coming into leaf, and the basilica appears above the treetops long before you reach it.

Near the top the way follows the road named after the funicular of Superga, the rack tramway from Sassi. According to Wikipedia, it opened in 1884 as a cable-hauled funicular and was rebuilt as a rack railway in 1934; it climbs 425 metres in a little over three kilometres. From the slopes the city and the river lay far below between the trees.

The way down runs by Moncanino through oak woods, past a dead trunk riddled with woodpecker holes, and back to San Mauro and across the plain under the weeping willows.
