Forty-one kilometres and 1,374 metres of climbing on the cross-country bike, a loop over the last mountains before the plain, south-west of Turin: up Monte San Giorgio above Piossasco, down to Cumiana, and back over the ridge by Truc Mongrosso to Sangano.

The climb to San Giorgio goes up through the woods on a stony trail, the last stretch in old snow, to a summit of about 840 metres with a big cross and the ruins of a chapel. Benedictine monks built a church and a small monastery up here in the tenth century, and since 2004 the mountain has been a nature park. From the top the plain lies open: Turin and its hill to the east, the Alps white all along the north, and the haze filling the distance. Riders on Gulliver say the views make up for the climb.

The way down drops towards Cumiana, past a yellow church in the hamlet of Allivellatori, on frosted grassy tracks and trails deep in dry leaves. Then the second climb, to the highest point of the day, about 870 metres on the ridge near Truc Mongrosso, where the lakes of Avigliana showed in the valley below through the trees.

The last trails run down through the pines towards Sangano, the plain glowing beyond the trunks.
