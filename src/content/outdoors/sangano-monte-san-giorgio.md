Nearly sixteen kilometres and 939 metres of climbing on foot, from Sangano up the long ridge to Monte San Giorgio and back by the woods above Trana.

The way up leaves the road by a stone pillar and climbs through woods only just coming into leaf, the slopes still bare and brown higher up. The paths wind past grey rock faces, boulders wrapped in roots and odd weathered pillars of stone, past a cluster of old stone huts by a meadow, and under a huge hollow chestnut, its trunk split and twisted with age.

Higher up the trees thin out onto dry grass and pines, and the views open: the valley below with its two rounded green peaks, the plain fading into the haze, and to the north a long line of snowy mountains. An Italian flag stands on a cairn along the ridge, and the top of Monte San Giorgio has its cross on a rock, a white arch on the grass and a radio mast among the trees.

The way back follows the ridge through bare oak woods scattered with boulders and standing stones, over a rocky crest with the plain below, then drops steeply on narrow paths towards the rooftops and through the fresh green woods back to Sangano.
