Nearly sixteen kilometres and 553 metres of climbing on foot, spread over two days: along the lake of Place Moulin to the Prarayer hut, a night there, and a morning walk up into the clouds before the way back.

The walk starts at the dam, a great curving wall of concrete 155 metres high and 678 metres long, which the village of Bionaz counts among the largest dams in Europe. Behind it the lake runs for about four kilometres up the valley, an unreal turquoise under the July sky, and the track follows its shore almost flat: past larches standing alone against the water, waterfalls dropping down the cliffs on the far side, fireweed in flower along the slope and a stretch cut into the rock under an overhang. At the head of the lake the water was low, leaving pale banks of gravel, and the valley opened out into pastures where cows grazed among the larches below the glaciers.

The Prarayer hut stands at about 2,000 metres at the end of the lake, among the stone huts of the alpine pastures, with flowers on the balcony and the lake showing down the valley.

The next morning the weather had turned. The path climbed out of the larches into the mist, over grass and stones and past old snow in a gully, following yellow waymarks from rock to rock up to about 2,400 metres. For a while there was nothing but cloud, then it began to tear open: the dark ridges came and went, a stream crashed down a gully of loose rock, and finally the head of the valley appeared, glaciers and a braided stream below the drifting cloud.

The way down crosses the torrent on a wooden footbridge among the larches and joins the meadows of Prarayer again. On the track back along the lake a white goat came walking up to meet me, an old wooden hut looked down over the water, and a last waterfall poured into a rocky pool before the dam.
