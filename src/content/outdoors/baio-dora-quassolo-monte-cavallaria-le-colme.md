Fifteen kilometres and 1,414 metres of climbing on foot, from Baio Dora at the mouth of the Aosta valley up through Quassolo to Monte Cavallaria and on along the ridge to Le Colme, and back down through the woods.

The way up starts between the stone houses of the village and follows the old mule tracks: paths between high drystone walls, cobbled ramps, and steps cut straight into the slabs of bare rock. Higher up they run through the woods, past ruined huts and boulders among the ferns, to the open ridge, where a viper lay coiled in the middle of the path.

Monte Cavallaria, 1,478 metres, is a paragliding take-off: a windsock stands on the grassy top, wings were laid out on the slope, and one pilot was already circling over the valley. From there the ridge goes on over rocky crests and boulder fields to Le Colme, 1,653 metres, its sign on the rocks and a cairn nearby. The cloud came and went along the crest, opening onto the valley of the Dora winding up towards Aosta, snowy peaks behind it, and on the other side onto the plain round Ivrea and its lakes.

The way down drops back into the woods, through birches and tall broadleaf trees, past roofless stone houses swallowed by the green and a rotten wooden footbridge, to Baio Dora.
