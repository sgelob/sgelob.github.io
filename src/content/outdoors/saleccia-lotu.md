Just under four kilometres on foot, almost flat, on the coastal path from the beach of Saleccia to the beach of Lotu, in the Désert des Agriates, in August.

The way leaves the long white beach of Saleccia on sandy paths through the junipers and follows the rocky shore round the headland, through dry maquis and over bare granite, past a field of small cairns, with the sea a deep blue beyond the rocks. It ends at Lotu, a bay of turquoise water full of boats.

The Agriates have no roads to the sea: the beaches are reached by boat from Saint-Florent, on foot along the coast or by four-wheel drive, and the Conservatoire du littoral protects much of the land. It was for this emptiness that Saleccia was chosen in 1961 to film the D-Day landings in *The Longest Day*: according to the Corsican tourist board, there was no concrete on the shore, so it could pass for the beaches of Normandy.
