Just over nine kilometres and 822 metres of climbing on foot, from Caselette up to the top of Monte Musinè, the first mountain of the Val di Susa, and down over Monte Calvo, at the end of July.

The way climbs from the Brione side on gravel tracks and paths through pines and meadows, with the plain and its lakes spreading out in the haze below, and then up a ridge of grass, bare rock and burnt trees, where the mountains of the valley open to the west.

On the summit, at about 1,150 metres, stands a great white cross. According to Wikipedia, it is fifteen metres tall, in reinforced concrete, and was built in 1901; the regional tourism board links it to the battle fought at the foot of the mountain in 312 between Constantine and Maxentius.

The way down follows the steep, eroded east ridge, which Gulliver describes as the most walked route from Caselette, to Monte Calvo, a lower top where, according to Wikipedia, a Way of the Cross climbs from the village to a chapel, and down past the small church of Sant’Abaco to the plain.
