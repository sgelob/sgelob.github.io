Twelve kilometres and 311 metres of climbing on foot, a loop from Rivalba over the wooded tops of the Bric Turniola and the Bric del Cerro and back, in the middle of November.

The way leaves the village on cobbled streets with the hills misty beyond the gardens, and the castle of Rivalba stands on its hill above a field of young green wheat. Past a small chapel at a junction the lanes climb into the woods, where autumn was at its height: paths deep in fallen leaves, branches across the way and berries on the thorns.

On the slopes between the two brics the woods hide old farm buildings: sheds swallowed by ivy under a big oak, abandoned barns and a roofless brick ruin standing among the trees. Sunken paths lead on through the leaves, past a meadow with a fence and glimpses of the wooded valleys below.

The way back comes down by lanes between the meadows and a row of poplars, and ends in the cobbled square by the brick tower and the town hall.
