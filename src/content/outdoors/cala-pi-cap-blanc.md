Nearly fourteen kilometres and 148 metres of climbing on foot, from Cala Pi along the cliffs of the south coast of Mallorca to Cap Blanc and back, on a hot August afternoon.

Cala Pi is a narrow inlet cut deep into the rock, with a small beach at its head packed with bathers, boat sheds along the water and green water under the pines. Its stone watchtower stands on the point above the bay. According to the Llucmajor tourism office, it was the last tower built along this stretch of coast, finished in 1663 against the corsairs from North Africa: the cove is so narrow and steep that landing there was hard, and the tower came later than the others.

From the cove the path climbs onto a bare limestone plateau and follows the cliffs west, past turquoise inlets, wind-bent bushes and a dry-stone hut on the rock. The cliffs grow higher towards the cape, dropping straight into a sea glittering in the sun.

At Cap Blanc a round stone watchtower stands on the cliff top, and beyond it the lighthouse, designed by Emili Pou and first lit on 31 August 1863, according to Fars de Balears. The way back runs inland on gravel roads and red earth paths through the pines.
