About five kilometres and 220 metres of climbing on the Alpe di Siusi, from Compaccio up to the Bullaccia and back. There is no recording of this walk: the route is rebuilt from where the photos were taken, from noon until the storm clouds came in, and the way back to Compaccio after the last one is my best guess.

Compaccio is where the cable car from Siusi arrives, at about 1,850 metres, on the western edge of what is called Europe’s largest high-altitude alpine pasture: some 56 square kilometres of meadows inside the Dolomites World Heritage area. Behind the village the Sciliar rises as one long wall, with the needle of Punta Santner at its end. I walked up the grassy slope past the Tschötsch Alm and reached the mountain inn at the top of the Bullaccia chairlift, at a little over 2,100 metres.

Right behind the lift station is the Engelrast, a viewing platform with benches turned towards the Dolomites, and across the pasture stand the towers of the Sassolungo group. I stopped there for a long while. When I set off again, north across the plateau among grazing cows, the sky had changed: dark clouds sat on the Sciliar and over the Sassolungo, and I turned back towards Compaccio.

The full Bullaccia round trail, signposted from Compaccio, is about 9 kilometres and three hours. It goes on past the Fillner Kreuz to the Witches’ Benches, two big porphyry boulders at the plateau’s western edge where, local legend says, witches used to meet. This walk took only its first part.
