Four kilometres and 207 metres of climbing on foot, a short loop from the Col de Bavella up among the granite needles, in August.

The pass lies at 1,218 metres, and a few metres from the road a statue of Notre-Dame-des-Neiges stands on a mound of boulders. From there a track leads into the pines and a rocky path climbs between granite walls and eroded boulders, with cairns marking the way, to a ridge facing the needles: jagged towers of pale rock above a forest that rolls away in blue valleys towards the sea.

The trees here are laricio pines, endemic to Corsica, tall and straight on the slopes and twisted on the ridges, their roots spread over the bare rock. The way back drops through the pine wood to the pass.
