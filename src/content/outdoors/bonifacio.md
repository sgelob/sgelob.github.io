Five kilometres and 90 metres of climbing on foot, round Bonifacio at the southern tip of Corsica, in August.

The way starts at the harbour, at the end of the long narrow inlet where the boats lie moored below the cliffs, and climbs to the old town through an arched passage and lanes below the walls, past the bastion. Out on the plateau beyond the houses a dusty path runs past ruined windmills to the edge of the cliffs. From up there the white limestone drops sheer into the sea, the houses of the old town stand right on the edge of it.

Cut into the cliff below the walls is the Staircase of the King of Aragon. Legend says its 187 steps were carved in a single night by the troops of Alfonso V of Aragon during the siege of 1420, and that the defenders drove them back at the top. In reality, according to the Porto-Vecchio tourist office, the stairs were cut to reach a freshwater well at the foot of the cliff.
