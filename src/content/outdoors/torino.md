Twelve kilometres and 47 metres of climbing through Turin on a rainy Saturday in January, a city walk that stayed mostly under the arcades.

Turin is built for days like this: the city has around eighteen kilometres of porticoes, and the way went from one to the next with the shutters still down and the stone floors shining. From the centre it ran down to the avenues of the Valentino, bare plane trees and puddles all the way, and along the Po, grey and swollen, behind its green iron railings.

Back across the river and up towards the centre, the last stop was Piazza Castello, so wet it worked as a mirror. Above it stands the Torre Littoria, built in 1933 and 1934, some eighty-seven metres of brick and stone over the arcades.
