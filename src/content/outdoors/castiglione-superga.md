Twenty-seven and a half kilometres and 627 metres of climbing on the cross-country bike: a short loop over the top of the Turin hill and back along the river.

The way up goes round the east side of the hill first, through Cordova and the farms above Pavarolo, on tracks of white gravel cut between earth banks, then joins the road to Superga for the last stretch to the basilica, at about 670 metres the highest point of the day.

On 2 September 1706, with Turin under French siege, Duke Victor Amadeus II and his cousin Prince Eugene of Savoy climbed this hill to look down on the enemy lines and plan the attack. The Duke vowed a church to the Virgin if the city was saved; the battle of 7 September lifted the siege, and Juvarra’s basilica is the vow kept. From the wall below it the whole city lies open, with the Po shining through it and the Alps behind.

Then the long descent on the Strada di Superga, the old road down through the woods to Sassi and the river, almost 450 metres in six kilometres, and back along the Po through San Mauro.
