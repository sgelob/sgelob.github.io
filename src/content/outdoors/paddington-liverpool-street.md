Eleven kilometres and 92 metres of climbing on foot, across London from Paddington to Liverpool Street on a grey March day: through Hyde Park to Westminster, along the river and into the City.

The way crosses Hyde Park under bare trees, with geese on the grass, and comes out at Hyde Park Corner. On top of the Wellington Arch stands a bronze chariot drawn by four horses, with a winged figure of Peace descending on it. English Heritage calls it Europe’s largest bronze sculpture; it was hoisted onto the arch in January 1912. Then down past the red forecourt of Buckingham Palace to Big Ben and Parliament.

From Westminster the walk follows the Embankment past the London Eye, crosses Trafalgar Square below Nelson’s Column and goes along the Strand, where the red buses pass the stone fronts. At Blackfriars the river opens out, boats moored below the towers of the City, and a narrow street frames the dome of St Paul’s. The last stretch passes the Bank junction among the glass towers and ends at Liverpool Street station.
