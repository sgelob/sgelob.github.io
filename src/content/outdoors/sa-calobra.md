Five kilometres and 156 metres of climbing on foot, a short walk from the cove of Sa Calobra to the mouth of the Torrent de Pareis and back, on a bright August morning.

Sa Calobra is reached by one of the most famous roads on the island, a ribbon of hairpins dropping to the sea that several sources credit to the engineer Antonio Parietti, built in the early 1930s. At the bottom, boats lie at anchor in the deep blue water below a rocky headland.

From the cove a paved path runs along the cliffs and through tunnels cut in the rock, about two hundred metres of them in all according to one guide, and then a rock arch opens onto the turquoise water. On the other side the Torrent de Pareis reaches the sea between towering walls, its pebble beach crowded with bathers. In summer, when the riverbed is dry, the gorge can be walked inland between the cliffs, and the way went a little way up it among the grey boulders before turning back.
