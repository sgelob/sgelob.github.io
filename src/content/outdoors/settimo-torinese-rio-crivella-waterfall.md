Nearly nineteen kilometres and 512 metres of climbing on foot, from Settimo over the Po and up into the hill above Castiglione to the waterfall of the Rio Crivella, and back down by San Mauro.

The walk starts flat: across the river on a footbridge, the water so still that the trees on the bank stood mirrored in it, then along a lane between a meadow and the maize. At the foot of the hill a road climbs under an old stone wall, and a sunken lane between ivy-covered banks leads up past Tetti Varetto to a dry meadow with the wooded hills rolling away behind.

From there the path drops into the Valle Garavaglia, through a tall wood of slender trunks and down sunken trails towards the stream. The Rio Crivella is a small thing, but walking guides call it the only stream on the Turin hill that makes a true waterfall, falling into a narrow gorge with steep walls of conglomerate laid down in the late Oligocene. Walkers on the trail point out that the path crosses the stream several times, and that the fall is at its best after rain. In July it was only a thin veil, but the gorge was cool and green, with moss over the boulders and a great overhang of the same pebbly rock above the trail.

The way out climbs by Tetti Francesi on a stony path between tall grass, past a jay’s blue-barred feather lying on the dry ground, and up to Tetti None. Then it’s down across the slopes towards San Mauro, with the basilica of Superga showing on its hill between the trees, along the lanes of Sambuy under old walls and hedges, and back across the plain beside an irrigation channel to Settimo.
