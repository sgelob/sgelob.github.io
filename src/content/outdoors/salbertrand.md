Five kilometres and 99 metres of climbing on foot, a short loop round Salbertrand in the upper Val di Susa, at the end of July.

The way crosses the meadows below the mountains, past hay bales and grazing cows, follows a dry stony streambed up towards the peaks and comes back on gravel tracks through the pines. In the village it winds through narrow cobbled lanes between old stone houses, past a small chapel at a corner and a courtyard full of flowers.

Salbertrand has a place in Waldensian history. In the summer of 1689 the Waldensians, exiled to Switzerland a few years before, marched back over the mountains to their valleys, in what they call the Glorious Return. Here, by the Dora, about a thousand of them found the only bridge held by French troops more than twice their number, forced it and went on: the battle of Salbertrand, the hardest fight of the march.
