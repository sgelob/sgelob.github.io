Fifteen kilometres and 135 metres of climbing on foot, a loop out of the northern edge of Swindon onto the low ridge above Blunsdon and along the old lanes to Kingsdown.

The walk leaves the new estates on footpaths along green verges and shaded lanes beside the parks, then climbs gently through tunnels of hedge and over the dual carriageway, which cuts through the green fields below. At the edge of Broad Blunsdon an old carved milestone stands among the nettles, its lettering worn almost smooth; it is one of several listed milestones around Blunsdon, left from the days of the turnpike roads.

Beyond the village the paths cross wide fields of tall grass under a sky streaked with vapour trails, through wooden gates in the hedges and along fences, and follow Kingsdown Lane east. A heritage trail leaflet from the council calls it an ancient Saxon path along the ridge, and it still feels like one: a narrow way between hedges and a wood of twisted trunks, with meadows of wildflowers opening at the side.

The way back runs along the ridge, with the vale stretching away below, and down a wide green slope with the rooftops of Swindon below.
