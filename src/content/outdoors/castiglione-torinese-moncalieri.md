Fifty-one and a half kilometres and 798 metres of climbing on the cross-country bike, along the Turin hill from end to end as far as Moncalieri, and back along the Po.

From Cordova the hill drops away to the plain, hazy in the late-summer heat. The route climbs to Monte Aman on forest tracks under tall conifers and follows the ridge south past Cento Croci and Pecetto, then down through Revigliasco to the hamlets on the Moncalieri side of the hill: lanes between old brick walls, a narrow sandy trail through the scrub, and a field of tall plants in yellow flower below a valley of greenhouses.

Moncalieri was one of the favourite residences of the House of Savoy, and its castle has been a World Heritage Site since 1997, with the other royal residences round Turin. It was there, in November 1849, that Victor Emmanuel II signed the Proclamation of Moncalieri, dissolving the Chamber so that a new one would approve the peace with Austria.

The way back is flat: through Testona and along the Po through the city, on the white gravel of the riverside paths past Sassi and the Meisino.
