Seventy-two kilometres and 1,500 metres of climbing on the cross-country bike: over the Turin hill to Chieri, round through the fields to Moncalieri, then back up and along the whole ridge.

The way out drops to the Po and climbs again from the city side, past Mongreno to Cento Croci, then down the other side of the hill into Chieri. The town grew from the hill where the church of San Giorgio stands, rebuilt by 1442 after a fire, and from up there its roofs and old brick towers spread out below, with the Turin hill green behind them. In the Middle Ages its cloth trade made it rich, and it was called the city of a hundred towers.

South of the town the route leaves the tarmac for farm tracks between maize stubble and vegetable fields, through Madonna della Scala and Valle Sauglio, to Testona on the hill above Moncalieri. In 1228 people from Testona founded Moncalieri itself, as a refuge from the raids of Chieri, the town the ride had just come from.

From Testona the Strada Santa Brigida climbs back into the hill, more than 400 metres in a few kilometres, and someone had sprayed WELCOME TO DISAGIO, welcome to discomfort, on a small building at the side of the road. At the top, the Colle della Maddalena at about 713 metres, the city lay below with the Alps behind it. The way back follows the ridge, past the Eremo, Cento Croci and the Via dei Colli to Superga, before the descent off the hill.
