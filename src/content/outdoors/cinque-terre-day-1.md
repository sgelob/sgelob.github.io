Seventeen kilometres and about 620 metres of climbing along the coast, from Levanto to Corniglia: the first of two days on the Cinque Terre in September 2015.

From Levanto the path climbs through holm oaks, past little cairns of flat stones, and crosses the Punta Mesco headland, the green wall that closes the bay of Monterosso to the west. On its tip stand the ruins of the hermitage of Sant’Antonio, which most sources date to the 13th or 14th century. Some of the photos of the headland are from an earlier walk on the same path, in July 2014.

Then the long descent to Monterosso and its beach, with agaves above the sand. After the village the path becomes the Sentiero Azzurro, trail number 2: stone steps between dry-stone walls, and at every bend the sea. Vernazza shows up first from above, its harbour guarded by the round tower of the castle and the domed bell tower of the church.

From Vernazza the trail climbs again through olive groves and terraces until Corniglia appears on its ridge, the one village of the five that does not sit on the sea. The day ended down the Lardarina, the long zigzag stairway of a few hundred steps from the village to the station.
