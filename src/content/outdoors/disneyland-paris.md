Sixteen and a half kilometres and 32 metres of climbing on foot in two days at Disneyland Paris: under six kilometres on the first afternoon and almost eleven on the second. Nobody goes to a theme park for a walk, but the tracker says it is one.

The map shows what a day in the park looks like: loops that start from the gates, go round the lands and come back to the central plaza again and again. The second day’s line is almost twice as long as the first.

In Frontierland the red rock spires of Big Thunder Mountain rise from the middle of a river. The Paris version is the only one that Disney built on an island: the trains leave the station on the bank and reach the mountain through a tunnel under the water. Next to it stands a row of yellow adobe houses with barred windows and wooden beams.

From inside the park a striped balloon on a cable shows above the autumn trees, hanging over the lake outside the gates.
