Fifteen kilometres and 879 metres of climbing on foot, on the island of Krk: from Batomalj above the bay of Baška over the bare plateau to Stara Baška on the sea, and back up to Obzova, the highest point of the island.

I set off before sunrise, on stony tracks between drystone walls and through the pines above the valley, and the sun came up over the bay of Baška and the mountains of the mainland as the path reached the edge of the plateau. Up there the land is bare karst: dry grass, rocks, a few pines bent by the wind, walls running in every direction and the islands of the Kvarner floating in the haze.

The way down to Stara Baška follows gullies of pale rock that open, one after another, onto the sea. The village is a few streets of houses with red roofs on a small bay, with pebble coves and clear water below the slope.

From the sea the path climbs back onto the plateau, past sheep grazing among the stones, to Obzova, 568 metres. Walkers on Komoot warn that this is a hard loop, on exposed karst with almost no shade, and by then the sun was high. Obzova is the only summit of Krk on the Croatian mountaineering trail, and from the top the view takes in the whole of the Kvarner, from the wind turbines on the mainland ridges to the islands in the haze.

The way back crosses the plateau past stone enclosures and an old concrete bunker, and drops through the woods to Batomalj. Below it, in Jurandvor, stands the church of St Lucy, where the Baška tablet was found in 1851: a slab carved in Glagolitic around 1100, one of the oldest texts in Croatian.
