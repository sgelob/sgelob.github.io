Thirty-six kilometres and 1,328 metres of climbing on the cross-country bike, a loop round the hill: east past Gassino and Bardassano to Sciolze and Montaldo, then west over the top by Superga and down to San Mauro.

It was a frosty winter day. The gravel lanes towards Bardassano ran between fields white with frost, the hills fading into the mist beyond them, and in the woods below Sciolze a huge conifer, a sequoia by the look of it, towered over the bare trees around it.

From Montaldo and Pavarolo quiet roads climb west to the ridge and on towards Superga, where the city lies spread out below the parapet, flat and grey under a clear sky.

The way down to San Mauro leaves the tarmac for the woods on the north side of the hill: muddy paths down wet gullies, fallen branches across the line, and dry leaves deep on the last tracks. Riders on Gulliver warn that the paths down this side of the hill turn steep and slippery after rain, and they had not dried out.
