Twenty-eight kilometres and 1,289 metres of climbing on the trail bike, a loop on the trails of the hill from Gassino round to San Mauro, much of it in the woods.

The first part climbs onto the ridge towards Bussolino and Bardassano, on a path past piles of cut branches with the hills opening up through the bare trees, then over an open hilltop and patches of pale chalky ground towards Pavarolo. Spring had only just reached the woods: most trees still bare, the first leaves a pale green haze, and the floor of the valleys covered in the leaves of wild garlic, not yet in flower.

From Pavarolo the trails wind west through the woods above Rivodora, past a fallen trunk across the path, a wooden boardwalk over a muddy gully and a rocky stream bed, then climb again on paths deep in dry leaves to Moncanino, where the brick tower shows through the branches.

Below it, by the Po, lies San Mauro, which grew up round the Benedictine abbey of Santa Maria di Pulcherada. The town’s old name, Pulchra Rada, is first written down in 991, and the church still keeps its twelfth-century bell tower. The way back runs east along the hill to Castiglione.
