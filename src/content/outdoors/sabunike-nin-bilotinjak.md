Twelve and a half kilometres and barely 77 metres of climbing on foot, a flat dawn walk round the lagoon of Nin, north of Zadar: from Sabunike along the shore to the old town and back by Bilotinjak.

I started before sunrise, on a path through the pines, and the sun came up behind the Velebit range across the sea. From there the way follows the lagoon: sandy shores and wide flats left by the low water, pine-topped islets, and a wooden boardwalk across the salt marsh between the reeds. The beaches here are known for the mud of the lagoon, which people spread on themselves for its healing powers.

The old town of Nin stands on an islet in the lagoon, only about 500 metres across, joined to the mainland by two stone bridges from the sixteenth century. Boats lay still in the water below the bell tower. Inside the walls is the little church of the Holy Cross from the ninth century, often called the smallest cathedral in the world, from the time when Nin was the seat of a bishop.

The way back crosses to the open sea at Bilotinjak, past pebbly coves and a small harbour, and returns to Sabunike under the pines.
