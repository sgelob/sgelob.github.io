Twenty-two kilometres and 900 metres of climbing on the trail bike, a short loop over the hills between Castiglione, Gassino and Pavarolo, with more up and down packed into it than the distance suggests.

The way out climbs onto the ridge above Gassino and follows it on dirt, drops towards Bussolino on a waymarked footpath, then climbs again past Bardassano to Pavarolo before turning back over the hill.

The trails were dry and fast, but the woods were not doing well. After weeks without rain the leaves hung limp, branches had come down across the paths, and in the clearings elder and tall weeds had grown so thick that the single track was only a parting in the green. Brambles and thorny plants reached in from both sides, the mosquitoes and horseflies found every stop, and it was already hot.

Below Bardassano the woods open onto a valley, a road winding through it under a line of trees and the first sunflower fields coming into flower on the slopes.

Gassino gives its name to a stone from these hills: a limestone laid down in the Eocene sea, full of fossil red algae, shells and sea urchins. From the end of the seventeenth century to the middle of the nineteenth it was the most used sedimentary stone in Turin, for plinths, columns and fireplaces in its churches and palaces.
