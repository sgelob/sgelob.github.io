Fifteen kilometres and 677 metres of climbing on foot, from Balangero up over the wooded tops of Monte Giovetto, Rolei and San Vittore and round the rim of the old asbestos mine to the Bric Forcola.

The walk leaves through the cobbled streets of the old town and climbs straight into the woods: grassy paths, tall pines and ferns, then stony tracks of green-grey rock between the pines and the broom. Higher up the trees open onto the tops, with a green mountain standing over the valley on one side, the plain stretching away on the other, and the snowy peaks of the Lanzo valleys along the horizon.

From the ridge the old mine comes into view, a great stepped pit cut into the side of San Vittore. Chrysotile asbestos was dug here from around 1917 until 1990, when the company went bankrupt; in its time it was the largest asbestos mine in western Europe, and today the whole area is a site of national interest under environmental remediation. The track runs across the grey plateaus of the old spoil heaps, past slabs of rock and young birches, and looks down into the pit, where a lake about 45 metres deep has filled the bottom, blue-green below the terraced walls. Danger signs on the trees warn of falls and landslides along the edge.

The way down drops through thin woods and tall grass, past a giant conifer towering over a fence and along a lane through the meadows, through ferns and beside a stream running under a rock wall. The last stretch comes back towards Balangero past a small memorial with old guns and down a long stairway towards the town.
