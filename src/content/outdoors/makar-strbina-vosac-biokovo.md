Thirteen kilometres and 1,233 metres of climbing on foot, straight up the wall of Biokovo above Makarska: from the village of Makar to Vošac, past the Štrbina viewpoint, and down through Kotišina.

I set off in the dark, along the lamplit lanes of Makar, and the first light found me on the old stone path climbing below the grey cliffs, with Makarska and the sea already far below. The path winds up through pines and scree, between drystone walls and narrow rocky gullies, with a fox slipping through the trees on one of the slopes.

Vošac, at 1,421 metres, sits right on the seaward edge of the plateau and is one of the finest viewpoints of the range: a stone hut on the top, the coast and the islands below, and behind it the bare karst of Biokovo rolling away inland. On the plateau horses grazed among the rocks, a foal with them.

The way down follows the ridge back towards the coast, past juniper and trail markers painted on the boulders, and drops through pine woods and boulder-filled gullies to Kotišina, under rock faces lit by the sun.
