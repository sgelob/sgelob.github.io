Nearly thirteen kilometres and 91 metres of climbing on foot, a loop through central London on a bright March day: along the river to Westminster, through the parks to Hyde Park Corner, and back through Soho and Covent Garden to the Thames.

The way out follows the river past the National Covid Memorial Wall, which runs along the embankment opposite the Houses of Parliament. Volunteers began painting its red hearts in March 2021, one for each life lost, and bereaved families have been writing names and messages on them ever since; in 2025 the government said it would be preserved. Across the river Big Ben stood over Westminster, with the London Eye turning behind it.

Through the parks the first crocuses were out in the grass under the plane trees, and at Hyde Park Corner the arch carries its bronze statue over the traffic, near a pavilion of bronze airmen. The way back wanders through the streets of Soho, under the red lanterns of Chinatown, past old shopfronts and the glass arch of a hall in Covent Garden, and comes back to the river as the light turned gold over the towers of the City.
