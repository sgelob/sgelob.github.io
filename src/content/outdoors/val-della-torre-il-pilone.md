Twelve kilometres and 877 metres of climbing on foot, from the hamlets above Val della Torre up to the Pilone, the white votive pillar on the ridge above the valley, and back down the old mule tracks.

The way starts among young trees and birches beside a stream, then climbs past bright green meadows and on old stone paths across slopes of dry grass and pines, with big clouds sailing over the mountains. Higher up the path crosses a field of reddish scree, and in the dry grass the first blue gentians were out.

Above the last larches the slopes were still in their winter colours, long sweeps of pale grass and bare trees, with stone huts and drystone walls from the old pastures. The Pilone stands at about 1,528 metres, a small pillar that can be seen from Val della Torre, and walkers on Gulliver note that it keeps a summit book. From the ridge the valley falls away to the plain on one side and the rocky mountains of the Lanzo valleys rise on the other, half hidden under the cloud.

The way down follows paved mule tracks between drystone walls and birches, past a stream running over slabs of rock and a fire salamander in the wet grass, back to the leafy lanes above the village.
