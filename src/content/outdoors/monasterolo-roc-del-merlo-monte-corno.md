Eleven kilometres and 760 metres of climbing on foot, from Monasterolo up the ridge to the Roc del Merlo and Monte Corno, at the mouth of the Lanzo valleys, and back down.

It was early spring: a bush of yellow flowers by the path, the birches still bare, and a long procession of pine caterpillars crossing the track nose to tail. The way climbs through bare woods and up steep slopes of pale dry grass, with the plain spread out in the haze below.

Along the ridge the rocks break through, a pillar of stone among the birches and boulders piled on the crest, and the view opens north onto the snowy mountains of the Lanzo valleys. The Roc del Merlo comes first, then Monte Corno, about 1,226 metres, with its cross on the summit rocks looking out over the plain; walkers on Gulliver call the view of the snowy Lanzo peaks from up there stunning.

The way down follows the long dry slopes above the hazy valley, over brown rocks looking straight down on the rooftops, and back through a tall pinewood.
