Sixty-six kilometres and 1,524 metres of climbing on the cross-country bike, a long loop that takes in both sides of the Turin hill: down to the river and the edge of the city, over the top, and back across the farmland to the east.

The way out follows the Po through San Mauro to the Meisino, then turns up into the hill through Reaglie, climbing past Cento Croci to Pino. From there it drops down the Valle Balbiana on the far side, with a gravel bend at the foot of a steep wooded bank, and out to the edge of the plain towards Chieri, on narrow roads between hedges and dry grass under a grey, fleecy sky.

Then north-east through the fields: farm tracks beside the young maize towards Montaldo, and white roads between the wheat round Marentino, already turning gold, as the clouds broke up and the sky turned blue.

Marentino keeps one of the oldest churches in these hills, Santa Maria dei Morti, a Romanesque parish church that became the cemetery chapel when the village moved, hence its name. Its apse was painted in 1450 by Guglielmetto Fantini, a painter from Chieri, and it was declared a national monument in 1888.

The way back runs through Sciolze and Rivalba, past Bussolino and down through Gassino.
