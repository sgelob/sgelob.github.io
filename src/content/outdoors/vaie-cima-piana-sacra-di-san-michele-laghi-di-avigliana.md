Forty-two kilometres and 1,299 metres of climbing on the cross-country bike, from Vaie at the bottom of the Susa valley up to the ridge of Cima Piana, along to the Sacra di San Michele and down to the lakes of Avigliana.

The climb starts above Sant’Antonino on a narrow road lined with little piles of stones, the valley opening below and snow white on the peaks across it. Higher up it turns into forest tracks through woods in full autumn colour, birches among them, the leaves golden on the ground and the shadows long across the way.

At the top, about 1,460 metres near Cima Piana, a grassy track runs along the ridge among the larches, with the valley lost in the haze below. From there the way goes east past Colle Braida and down to the foot of the Sacra di San Michele, the abbey on the summit of the Pirchiriano that has watched over the mouth of the valley since it was founded in the 980s. Umberto Eco drew on it for The Name of the Rose, and since 1994 it has been the symbol of Piedmont.

Then down to the lakes of Avigliana and back west along the valley floor through Sant’Ambrogio and Chiusa di San Michele, with the Sacra on its ridge above the road.
