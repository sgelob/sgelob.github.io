Forty-eight kilometres and 1,601 metres of climbing on the cross-country bike, back and forth across the Turin hill: west to San Mauro, up over the top and down the city side, then east again through Pino and Baldissero.

The first stretch runs west along the slopes towards San Mauro, on a narrow single track through the ivy and then a quiet road along the hillside. Above the woods by Sambuy rises the Torre di Moncanino, a tall neo-Gothic tower of red brick with a metal angel on top that shows the way of the wind. It dates from 1857 and is often credited to Alessandro Antonelli, the architect of the Mole, though recent studies have doubted it; it belongs to the nearby Villa Soley, and the story goes that guests at the villa’s parties in the late nineteenth century climbed it for the view over the hills.

From there the road climbs past Il Pilonetto to the top of the hill, then drops down the city side through Reaglie, where a lane forks round a grassy island. Below the ridge the route wanders on dirt tracks under the trees, scattered with fallen seeds, past a sculpture of crossed steel beams standing in the tall grass, then along narrow lanes between brick gateposts towards Mongreno, and a single track that follows a fence through the ivy almost down to the edge of the city.

Then back up to Cento Croci and along the Via dei Colli to Pino, east through Valle Ceppi and Baldissero, and over Cordova before the last descent.
