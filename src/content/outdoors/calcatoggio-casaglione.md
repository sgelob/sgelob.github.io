Seventeen kilometres and 441 metres of climbing on foot, a loop over the hills of Calcatoggio and Casaglione above the Gulf of Sagone, in the middle of August, set off in the dark before dawn.

The way climbs from the shore on quiet roads and then on dirt tracks through the maquis, with the mountains outlined against the first light. From the rocky hilltops the bay of the Liscia and its village appeared far below, and the gulf opened out towards the sea as the sun rose over the mountains. The tracks run on along the open ridges, past a gate and an old oak with a bench beneath it, then down through a grove of olive trees to the plain of the Liamone and the sea.

This is a loop of castles. Above Tiuccia and the beach of the Liscia, on a rocky summit that is hard to reach, stands the Castellu di Cinarca, a key place in Corsican medieval history, according to the Corsican tourist board. Near it are the ruins of the castle of Capraja, one of the last residences of the Cinarchesi, the medieval lords of the area: a ruined tower, walls against the sky and a vaulted room with the sea framed in its doorway.

The way back follows the coast, past empty beaches, granite rocks in clear water and a long curve of sand below the hills.
