Forty-eight and a half kilometres and 1,227 metres of climbing on the cross-country bike, a loop east across the farmland to Marentino and Andezeno and back over the top of the Turin hill, on one of those clear November days when the Alps stand out white all along the horizon.

The way out climbs past a brick wayside shrine, the road edged with fallen leaves, and over the hill by Pavarolo, where a village sits on an autumn slope below the snowy peaks. Then down into the open country: paved lanes along the railings across the meadows towards Montaldo and Marentino, the low sun throwing long shadows, a lone tree standing in the middle of a field.

At Avuglione, one of Marentino’s hamlets, the trees grow out of the top of a pale sandstone cliff, and from the edge of the village the view opens over the farmland and the plain to the whole chain of the Alps. In Andezeno a cobbled ramp runs down between a brick parapet and an ivy-covered wall.

The way back crosses a ploughed field and a field of cabbages towards Baldissero and Pino, with the pyramid of Monviso far off above the autumn woods, then climbs along the ridge to Monte Aman and on towards Superga. From the road below the basilica Turin lay in full below, the Po curving through it, with the snowy Alps behind. On the last stretch home a flock of birds crossed the deep blue sky.
