Eight and a half kilometres and 42 metres of climbing on foot, across Paris from the Gare de Lyon to the Eiffel Tower, almost all of it along the Seine, on a clear November afternoon that ended with the tower lit up.

The walk starts by going down to the Bassin de l’Arsenal, where houseboats are moored below a cobbled quay and the July Column of the Bastille closes the view. The basin was dug after the fortress was demolished in 1789, in place of the ditch that fed its moat from the Seine. It worked as a commercial port at the end of the Canal Saint-Martin until 1983, when the city turned it into a marina.

From the Pont de Sully the tip of the Île Saint-Louis stands out of the water like the bow of a ship. I followed the Quai de Béthune under the yellowing trees, then crossed to the Île de la Cité, where pixel mosaics sit in the stone of the quay wall below the souvenir shops.

Past the Louvre the light was already going. From the Cours la Reine the Eiffel Tower stood against an orange sky, and on the Pont Alexandre III a bronze nymph leans out over the river. The bridge was opened for the Exposition Universelle of 1900; the reliefs at the centre of its arch, the Nymphs of the Seine on one side and the Nymphs of the Neva on the other, are hammered copper by Georges Récipon.

On the left bank, by the Port des Invalides, a small electrical substation has a frowning face in purple tiles above its door. A little further the tower appears behind the onion domes of the Russian Orthodox Holy Trinity Cathedral, designed by Jean-Michel Wilmotte and finished in 2016. The walk ends in the gardens below the tower, with its beam already turning.
