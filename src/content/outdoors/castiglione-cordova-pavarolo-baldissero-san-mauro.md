Twenty-two kilometres and 1,005 metres of climbing on the trail bike, on my own, on a November day when the hill was more mud than trail.

It started above the fog. From the first ridge the valleys were full of it, with only the tops of the wooded hills showing through.

Along the ridge the woods were bare enough to let the light through. The paths were buried under leaves, pale and dry on the higher stretches, darker and wetter lower down, with a lone boulder sitting in them here and there and long shadows stretched across the slope.

Towards Bardassano the fog closed in again, and the road climbed into it with trees and houses only shapes at its edge. Bardassano has a castle that riders on Komoot call very striking from a distance, but not visitable; this time even the distance was hidden.

Past Bardassano the route crossed open fields towards Pavarolo, and this is where the mud really started. A grassy track between fruit trees had turned into a strip of brown slop above a valley still full of fog, and the farm tracks along the fields were cut into deep ruts by tractor tyres, soft and wet all the way down. Over a field of young green crops the sun was only a pale disc in the mist.

The way back ran through the woods again, on old tracks marked with yellow signs, sunken between banks of ivy and covered in leaves, with fallen branches arching over them, and a last climb through the bare trees.
