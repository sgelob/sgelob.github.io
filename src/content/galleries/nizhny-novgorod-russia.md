Nizhny Novgorod, where the Oka meets the Volga, seen across two seasons. In winter the city is white and quiet: lamps glowing over the frozen river at night, a lit Christmas tree among ice sculptures, red-brick Kremlin walls on a snowy slope, and trolleybuses and battered minibuses grinding along icy roads.

In summer everything is green. From the hillside the Kremlin towers look out over the wide river, with barges and cruise ships passing, and the long stairway climbs the slope. Along the embankment and in the parks there are war memorials, a tank, a gun, a fighter plane, steam locomotives and a boat on a pedestal, and white monastery churches with golden domes show among the trees.
