Dzerzhinsk, a factory town on the Oka in the Nizhny Novgorod region, seen in two seasons. In spring and summer there are pine woods, marshy meadows and a steel lattice tower rising among the trees. In winter everything goes white and quiet: skiers on forest tracks, a snow-roofed well, frost on window glass.

The town itself is Soviet through and through: panel apartment blocks, a mosaic-covered culture house, a red tram and yellow minibuses in the snow, the big city sign at the roundabout. Chimneys steam over the plant, rail tank cars stand rusting, and ruined halls sit beside wooden houses painted blue, with a stray dog or a cat on the roof.
