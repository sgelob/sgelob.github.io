Bruges on a bright day with big clouds, a city of brick, water and slow walking. Stepped gables line the cobbled lanes, ivy swallows whole houses, and the Belfry and the spire of the Church of Our Lady keep appearing at the end of every street.

I followed the canals, where tour boats slip under stone bridges and swans glide below willows, and found the quiet corners: a lace-curtained window with a little red devil, a black cat on the pavement, a red post box, a horse-head fountain.

On the market square the guild houses and the monument fill with people. There were waffles with cream and strawberries, and a windmill on its grassy mound at the edge of town.
